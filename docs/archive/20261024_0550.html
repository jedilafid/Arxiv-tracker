Are EEG Sequences Time Series? EEG Classification with Time Series Models and Joint Subject Training
Authors: Johannes Burchert, Thorben Werner, Vijaya Krishna Yalavarthi, Diego Coello de Portugal, Maximilian Stubbemann, Lars Schmidt-Thieme
First: 2024-04-10T12:24:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:32:05+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
As with most other data domains, EEG data analysis relies on rich domain-specific preprocessing. Beyond such preprocessing, machine learners would hope to deal with such data as with any other time series data. For EEG classification many models have been developed with layer types and architectures we typically do not see in time series classification. Furthermore, typically separate models for each individual subject are learned, not one model for all of them. In this paper, we systematically study the differences between EEG classification models and generic time series classification models. We describe three different model setups to deal with EEG data from different subjects, subject-specific models (most EEG literature), subject-agnostic models and subject-conditional models. In experiments on three datasets, we demonstrate that off-the-shelf time series classification models trained per subject perform close to EEG classification models, but that do not quite reach the performance of domain-specific modeling. Additionally, we combine time-series models with subject embeddings to train one joint subject-conditional classifier on all subjects. The resulting models are competitive with dedicated EEG models in 2 out of 3 datasets, even outperforming all EEG methods on one of them.

DISC: Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost for Time-Series Dataset
Authors: Yen-Ku Liu, Hongjie Chen, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan Rossi
First: 2026-10-22T13:52:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:52:09+00:00
Comments: Accepted as a conference paper at IEEE BigData 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The growth of time-series datasets and foundation models has made dataset comparison a central part of time-series workflows. Assessing source-target transfer before model adaptation requires similarity estimates that can summarize transfer-related dataset relationships without exhaustive source-target evaluation over every candidate pair. Many metrics have been proposed for measuring similarity between time-series datasets, but they capture different dataset relationships and vary in how well they align with downstream utility across tasks. We evaluate this gap by measuring the correlation between source-target similarity scores and downstream forecasting errors in out-of-distribution and transfer-learning settings. We propose Domain-Integrated Similarity Cost (DISC), a learnable aggregation framework that uses calibration transfer observations to select, weight, and combine similarity metrics for different downstream tasks. By integrating complementary metric values, DISC better distinguishes source-target dataset differences that matter for downstream behavior. After calibration, DISC scores source-target pairs using only their similarity-metric values and yields higher alignment than single-metric references on the reported split. These results show that time-series dataset similarity is task-dependent, and that learned metric integration provides a stronger proxy for downstream transfer behavior than manually selecting one similarity metric.

QuaNAS: Uncertainty-Aware Reuse of Earlier Searches for Query-Efficient Neural Architecture Search
Authors: Brama Seta Putra Satria, Dwi Sunaryono, Misbakhul Munir Irfan Subakti, Irzal Ahmad Sabilla, Laras Suciningtyas
First: 2026-10-22T13:30:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:30:25+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural architecture search (NAS) on a new dataset often affords only tens of short training runs, so what the search knows before its first evaluation decides its efficiency. Yet most NAS methods start from scratch, guided at best by zero-cost proxies that are unreliable across tasks. QuaNAS treats a library of earlier searches in the same search space as explicit knowledge of uncertain reliability. Main effects of design decisions learned from this library and, secondarily, zero-cost proxies become priors over candidate architectures without any label of the new dataset. A Gaussian process starts at the prior optimum and re-estimates after every evaluation how far each prior is trusted. Under a protocol frozen before any confirmation data were seen, QuaNAS was evaluated once on 12 new image, time-series, text and tabular datasets. It lowered anytime regret on the short-training objective 2.7- to 3.9-fold relative to five pre-specified NAS baselines that start from scratch (Holm-adjusted p = 0.0012 each). Its final models matched (non-inferiority margin 0.5 pp) or exceeded matched-recipe reference models on nine datasets, as did the prior optimum, selected without a single evaluation. Post-hoc comparisons with established transfer methods given the same library trace the gain to the library: without it, the same Gaussian process had over twice the regret. They also show where each transfer mechanism works best: the pooled main effects of QuaNAS for weakly transferable tabular tasks, per-task surrogates for image, time-series and text tasks and for small or mismatched libraries. Finally, the main effects make the transferred knowledge inspectable.

Abstract
Wearable inertial measurement unit (IMU) systems for construction worker human activity recognition (HAR) routinely report aggregate accuracies above 95 %, rarely under genuine subject-independent (leave-one-subject-out (LOSO)) evaluation, masking the per-individual variance that determines real-world deployment performance. Using the VTT-ConIoT benchmark, we conduct, to our knowledge, the first systematic configuration sensitivity analysis of this dataset: six classifiers across five sensor placements and three window sizes under a single LOSO protocol, a personalised-calibration pilot, and a validated comparison of tabular GAN-based and standard time-series augmentation. Per-subject LOSO accuracy for the main multi-sensor, 16-class configuration ranges from 61.9 % to 88.7 % (mean 77.5 %), a 27 pp spread, though all data are laboratory-collected. Multi-sensor fusion and longer windows both improve accuracy; standard augmentation gives small, consistent gains, while Conditional Tabular GAN (CTGAN) augmentation actively harms accuracy with a demonstrated distributional-fidelity mechanism; and personalised calibration recovers 16.8 pp for the weakest participants from just 30 % of their own data. These findings provide evidence-grounded configuration guidance for scaling occupational body sensor networks towards field deployment

Abstract
While current Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) provide robust control priors for humanoids, they only execute pre-defined reference motions. As a result, they are vulnerable to environmental shifts and incapable of reactive whole-body coordination. Naively cascading them with generative motion planners fails to achieve true reactivity, as inevitable tracking discrepancies induce fatal cumulative exposure bias. To bridge this gap, we propose ReactiveBFM, a real-time closed-loop planning-control framework. At its core, we effectively mitigate exposure bias via a scheduled prefix sampling curriculum, forcing the generative planner to actively learn error-recovery behaviors from imperfect physical states rather than ground-truth trajectories. Systematically, to reconcile the severe latency mismatch between auto-regressive planning and high-frequency tracking, we introduce an asynchronous replanning mechanism. Combined with trajectory chunking to temporally ensemble spatial references, our system guarantees spatio-temporally fluid execution without physical jitter. Deployed on the Unitree G1 humanoid, ReactiveBFM demonstrates unprecedented physical agility across a vast repertoire of text-conditioned closed-loop motions. Notably, ReactiveBFM achieves zero-shot moving target reaching, showcasing intricate whole-body coordination and on-the-fly replanning. In sim-to-sim benchmarking under severe perturbations, ReactiveBFM achieves a 93.1% success rate, significantly outperforming cascaded open-loop baselines by 28.6%.

Abstract
Soil moisture and temperature govern plant-water availability and microbial activity, making them key indicators for evaluating land-restoration efforts on degraded landscapes. However, the physical and data-driven models normally used to forecast them both need information that a newly instrumented restoration site does not yet have: hydraulic parameters for the former and months of per-site training data for the latter. We evaluate TimesFM 1, a 500M-parameter time-series foundation model, zero-shot on 15-minute telemetry from 27 sensor stations across three land-management regimes (Control, Treatment, Scald) at a semi-arid restoration site, forecasting 5 cm and 30 cm soil moisture and enclosure temperature at seven lead times from 1 h to 168 h. Using gap-aware rolling-origin evaluation across sensors, we find that TimesFM beats the best rule-based statistical forecasting baselines (persistence, seasonal-naive, or 7-day climatology) by 21-76% at sub-daily lead times but loses by 6-79% beyond a day, a pattern explained by a diurnal-alignment mechanism that holds for both soil-moisture depths and temperature. A CatBoost baseline trained walk-forward on the same origins with full history and weather covariates never wins overall, and we show that its weakness is partly a cold-start effect specific to short deployments. We additionally quantify the inference cost of zero-shot forecasting on commodity CPU hardware. Our results highlight a practical trade-off between zero-shot forecasting capability, predictive performance, and computational efficiency.

Abstract
AI models, and time-series models in particular are typically ranked by accuracy and deployed under constraints absent from that ranking. In this paper, we study a real setting where these constraints are explicit and measurable: handover pre- diction in cellular networks, where inference runs on the User Equipment once per 40 ms measurement period and must complete before the network acts. The timing bound is hard: a late prediction is superseded by the measurement it was predicting, while energy is a cumulative budget spent for the life of the device. Neither appears in the objective these models are selected on. We evaluate eight architectures under a protocol that screens for deadline feasibility at the tail rather than the mean, then ranks the feasible models on measured inference energy, count- ing the feature computation that precedes the forward pass and is usually omitted. Gradient-boosted trees on handcrafted temporal features are the most accurate model (F1=0.974) and among the cheapest to run, while the neural architecture an accuracy-only benchmark would rank first (CNN-1D, F1=0.914) costs over 3 times more energy per inference under a realistic duty-cycled deployment pattern. We give three conditions under which this outcome should be expected, so the finding applies beyond telecommunications. Accuracy-only benchmarking does not merely under-report deployment cost. It can rank first a model that cannot be deployed.

Abstract
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) use self-supervised pretraining to learn reusable representations for downstream tasks such as multivariate time series classification (MTSC). We examine how much pretrained weights contribute beyond the architecture itself by comparing seven publicly released instances from four TSFM families with architecture-matched randomly initialized counterparts on the UEA MTSC archive. For each instance, five random initializations are evaluated using four classifiers on frozen representations: ridge classification, logistic regression, RBF SVM, and random forest. After model-specific exclusions due to computational constraints, the study comprises 183 paired dataset-level comparisons. Pretraining improves mean accuracy for all seven instances, but the gains are modest, ranging from 1.43 to 5.19 percentage points, with an overall average improvement of 2.58 points. Three of seven per-instance Wilcoxon signed-rank tests have raw $p$-values below 0.05, but only NuTime remains significant after Holm correction. A secondary pooled test across all 183 pairs gives $p < 0.001$, while the corrected per-instance tests remain the primary evidence. We also compare with ROCKET, Hydra, and Quant, which remain competitive without requiring large-scale pretraining. These findings suggest that MTSC evaluations should distinguish the effect of pretrained weights from that of the underlying architecture and should include both architecture-matched random initialization and strong non-pretrained baselines.

Abstract
Time series anomaly detection (TSAD) is a foundational problem in data mining, aiming to identify observations that deviate significantly from nominal temporal patterns. Abstract
Martian ionospheric ions can be accelerated away from Mars by the solar wind, contributing substantially to atmospheric ion escape. Here, we develop a U-Net-based image segmentation model to identify a distinct population of escaping planetary ions, energetic O2+ beams, from a decade of Mars Atmosphere and Volatile EvolutioN (MAVEN) observations and perform an event-based statistical analysis of their global properties and dependence on external conditions. We find that O2+ beams are widely distributed throughout the Martian plasma environment, with a strong preference for the +E hemisphere, where the solar wind convective electric field points away from Mars. This pronounced spatial asymmetry indicates that the convective electric field plays a major role in beam formation. In the solar wind and magnetosheath, these beams are consistent with the plume. Their occurrence-weighted flux agrees with previous long-term plume estimates, whereas the flux during identified beam intervals is substantially higher. This highlights the advantage of our event-based approach in capturing the intrinsic properties of O2+ plumes. In the induced magnetotail, beam formation remains strongly associated with the convective electric field, while the Hall electric field can provide additional energization. The beam properties are primarily controlled by upstream solar wind conditions, with no apparent influence from solar extreme ultraviolet irradiance. These results might improve our understanding of ion escape at Mars. More broadly, we also demonstrate the potential of deep learning for identifying transient plasma populations from spacecraft observations.

Abstract
Recent time series foundation models (TSFMs) can consume covariates in-context, extending zero-shot forecasting to problems driven by external factors. This enables scenario analysis: forecasting how a target responds to events that set covariates to chosen future values. Whether they can do this reliably is an open question. We introduce WhatIf-Bench, a benchmark measuring how well forecasters use covariates to forecast a target's response to future events. WhatIf-Bench is semi-synthetic: time series come from mechanistic simulators of data centers, power grids, road traffic, and water networks, with controlled covariate-target relationships grounded in each system. Each task presents a history of events intervening on covariates, then asks to forecast a future event that either replays seen values (Replay) or sets them to unseen ones (Novel). Varying the number of covariates, historical events, and event length yields 37,665 tasks. Evaluating TSFMs, frontier LLMs, and simple regression baselines, we find the LLMs reach the highest skill (improvement over a naive baseline), ahead of the best TSFMs. Every model forecasts well on Replay futures (48 to 85% skill) but drops to -7 to 50% on Novel futures, with the regression baselines falling the most. Skill on Novel futures also falls as events set more covariates jointly. More historical events raise skill on Novel futures across the board, but large differences remain. WhatIf-Bench reveals generalization to Novel futures as the core challenge, and offers a testbed toward arbitrary scenario analysis.

Venue: NeurIPS 2026

Abstract
Causal discovery from nonlinear multivariate time series is challenging in high-dimensional systems, where the number of candidate directed relations grows quadratically with the number of variables. Existing methods are often limited by target-wise model fitting, repeated conditional testing, or expensive graph-search procedures. We propose SCDM, a shared-parameter meta-learning framework for scalable temporal causal discovery. SCDM treats each target variable as a task while learning a shared temporal predictor and a shared causal-strength matrix across tasks. Each meta-episode updates only a subset of target-variable tasks, allowing the model to accumulate structural evidence across the full system without exhaustive target-wise optimization. After training, a post-training graph readout converts the learned predictor into continuous directed causal scores for threshold-independent evaluation. We also provide a finite-error recovery principle showing that, under fully observed delayed-system assumptions and a positive risk-gap condition, thresholding SCDM scores recovers the graph when statistical, meta-optimization, and readout errors are below the population margin. Experiments on controlled synthetic, neuroimaging-inspired, high-dimensional, and real-world temporal benchmarks demonstrate competitive causal recovery and improved scalability in large temporal systems.

Synthesis and Verification of Register Automata over Data Sequences (Technical Report)
Authors: Chih-Duo Hong, Hongjian Jiang, Anthony W. Lin, Oliver Markgraf, Julian Parsert, Tony Tan
First: 2025-11-24T13:36:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T13:42:27+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Data sequences, such as time series, traces, and sensor streams, are words over large or infinite data domains. Their properties often depend on comparisons between values at different positions, which finite automata cannot express directly. We study synthesis and verification for Deterministic Register Automata (DRAs) over ordered data sequences. DRAs combine finite control with registers for remembered data values, providing compact symbolic descriptions of quantitative sequence languages. Our technical core is a local-search synthesis procedure and an exact robustness checker for DRAs. We fix the alphabet theory to ordered rationals $(\Q;<,=)$ with rational guard constants and use non-negative affine accumulator costs. For a fixed number of DRA registers, the checker runs in polynomial time; its dependence on that number is exponential. It reduces robustness to coverability and then to a shortest-path problem. Since the search ranges over $\Q^+$, it produces variable-length witnesses and, for RAA-definable alignment costs such as DTW, explores a counterexample space with no a priori length bound. As an application, we use learned DRAs as interpretable surrogates for queried neural sequence classifiers. A probably approximately correct (PAC) validation loop refines rejected candidates, returns genuine non-robustness witnesses for the classifier when found, and accepts a surrogate only with statistical guarantees for fidelity and local stability. We implement the framework in $\textsc{RAExc}$, enabling the synthesis of interpretable automata and the verification of their robustness. We evaluate the approach on benchmark languages and sequentialized MNIST, demonstrating high accuracy while verifying the robustness of the learned automata.

Abstract
Chain-of-thought (CoT) monitors are LLM-based systems that analyze reasoning traces to detect when outputs may exhibit attributes of interest, such as test-hacking behavior during code generation. In this paper, we use information-theoretic analysis to show that non-zero mutual information between CoT and output is a necessary but not sufficient condition for CoT monitorability. We identify two sources of approximation error that may undermine the performance of CoT monitors in practice: information gap, which measures the extent to which the monitor can extract the information available in CoT, and elicitation error, which measures the extent to which the monitor approximates the optimal monitoring function. Empirically, we show that CoT obfuscation can arise even in the absence of any optimization pressure against CoT. We further demonstrate that the fragility of CoT monitorability can be systematically mitigated through targeted training objectives. To this end, we propose a practical, label-free objective based on mutual information between outputs and CoTs, and benchmark it against an oracle-based method that directly rewards the monitored model for CoTs that maximize monitor accuracy. Across multiple environments, we show that our objective is effective at preventing CoT obfuscation even when training against a monitor, and helps mitigate reward hacking when the task reward is imperfectly specified.

Abstract
Financial crime detection requires identifying illicit activity within large transaction networks, where suspicious behaviour is rare and unfolds over time. Existing graph-based methods capture relationships among accounts but typically underuse the temporal behaviour encoded in account transaction histories. We introduce Temporal-Context Edge Enrichment (TCEE), a framework that addresses this gap by summarising each account's transaction history with a frozen time-series foundation model (TSFM), and enriching each transaction's representation with the relevant temporal context from these summaries. These enriched representations can be scored directly for edge classification, or fed into a graph neural network (GNN) as edge features to capture broader structural information. Across six AMLWorld datasets, TCEE improves PR-AUC over the strongest evaluated baseline on five by a relative 10.8% on average, while training 6.0x faster end-to-end. As TSFMs continue to advance, our results suggest they can strengthen financial crime detection by capturing temporal signals that complement graph-based approaches.

Abstract
This paper describes an approach to constructing nowcast and forecast predictive models for the occurrence of high-latitude decameter ionospheric irregularities based on SuperDARN and SECIRA radar data and space weather parameters. The method relies on self-learning pattern recognition in the dynamics of space weather parameters, that influence the irregularities formation. The proposed approach is a data-driven, multiscale method in which the spatial distribution of irregularities is approximated using a neural network of an original architecture, inspired by graph neural networks. The model was trained and tested using data from 11 radars during the period 2010-2025 (SuperDARN: CVW, ICE, ICW, INV, PGR, PYK, RKN, SAS, STO; SECIRA: EKB, MAGW). It is shown that the model achieves optimal performance when using geomagnetic coordinates MLT,MLAT. The optimal set of parameters for the best nowcast and forecast over studied set of parameters is a combination of geomagnetic and solar disturbance indices ($a_{p}$, $K_{p}$, $AE$, F10.7) from ground-based observations, and satellite-measured solar wind and interplanetary magnetic field parameters ($P_{sw}$, $E_{sw}$, $\widetilde{B_{z}}$, $T_{sw}$). Hourly mean values for all the parameters were used in the analysis. The results indicate that acceptable forecast quality is achieved with a lead time of up to 7-9 hours, with the highest quality reached for nowcast (zero lead time). It is shown that the parameters $\widetilde{B_{z}}$ and F10.7 are significant for nowcasting only and can be neglected for other lead times. It is shown that $AE$ and $T_{sw}$ are significant for mid-term forecast (\textgreater 2hours ahead) and can be neglected for short-term predictions. Statistical relationships between the maximum latitude of the equatorial boundary of the irregularities and space weather parameters are also discussed.

Abstract
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) perform unevenly across forecasting tasks. Recent work uses agentic model orchestration (AMO) to select or combine TSFMs, often outperforming individual forecasters. Yet these gains may reflect stronger or more complementary candidates rather than better orchestration. To disentangle these effects, we introduce FairAMO, a controlled evaluation protocol with frozen candidate forecasts. Experiments on GIFT-Eval show that AMO improves accuracy, but its gains are bounded by the candidate TSFMs. The best orchestrators reduce MASE by less than 6% relative to the best single TSFM across all tasks.
