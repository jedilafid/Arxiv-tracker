Covariance-Aware Transformers for Quadratic Programming and Decision Making
Authors: Kutay Tire, Yufan Zhang, Ege Onur Taga, Samet Oymak
First: 2026-02-16T06:39:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:48:47+00:00
Comments: Published in the proceedings of the Conference on Language Modeling (COLM), 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We explore the use of transformers for solving quadratic programs and how this capability benefits decision-making problems that involve covariance matrices. We first show that the linear attention mechanism can provably solve unconstrained QPs by tokenizing the matrix variables (e.g.~$A$ of the objective $\frac{1}{2}x^\top Ax+b^\top x$) row-by-row and emulating gradient descent iterations. Furthermore, by incorporating MLPs, a transformer block can solve (i) $\ell_1$-penalized QPs by emulating iterative soft-thresholding and (ii) $\ell_1$-constrained QPs when equipped with an additional feedback loop. Our theory motivates us to introduce Time2Decide: a generic method that enhances a time series foundation model (TSFM) by explicitly feeding the covariance matrix between the variates. We empirically find that Time2Decide uniformly outperforms the base TSFM model for the classical portfolio optimization problem that admits an $\ell_1$-constrained QP formulation. Remarkably, Time2Decide also outperforms the classical "Predict-then-Optimize (PtO)" procedure, where we first forecast the returns and then explicitly solve a constrained QP, in suitable settings. Our results demonstrate that transformers benefit from explicit use of second-order statistics, and this can enable them to effectively solve complex decision-making problems, like portfolio construction, in one forward pass.

Turning TS2Vec into a Foundation Model for Time Series Classification
Authors: Vasilii Feofanov, Zhenghan Tai, Andrei Ermakov, Irina Rish
First: 2026-10-20T17:47:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:47:51+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
TS2Vec is one of the strongest self-supervised methods for time series classification, introduced in 2022. It is designed to be trained anew on every dataset: self-supervised pre-training followed by linear probing. Since then, the field has shifted towards foundation models that reuse one encoder across datasets. Yet TS2Vec offers a compact convolutional architecture that operates directly on temporal samples and accepts variable-length inputs without resampling. We ask whether these properties can be retained when the encoder is pre-trained only once, and show that they can: TS2Vec can essentially be turned into a foundation model. Three changes suffice: pre-train on a synthetic corpus, feed the encoder a raw amplitude view and an instance-normalized shape view of the input through two small stems, and distill one specialist encoder per view into a single encoder. The result, TS2Vec-FM, is a 1.6M-parameter dilated convolutional network that, kept frozen, reaches 83.3% on UCR-128 and 75.3% on UEA-27 under linear probing, within 0.3 pp of MantisV2, the current state of the art, and above TS2Vec trained separately on each target dataset. What we find most interesting is how different TS2Vec-FM is from other foundation models in design: near-parity is reached without attention, without a patch tokenizer, and without intermediate-layer extraction, using an "old-school" convolutional encoder and a two-layer MLP stem.

Abstract
We present a method that models the evolution of an unbounded number of time series clusters by switching among an unknown number of regimes with linear dynamics. We develop a Bayesian non-parametric approach using a hierarchical Dirichlet process as a prior on the parameters of a Switching Linear Dynamical System and a Gaussian process prior to model the statistical variations in amplitude and temporal alignment within each cluster. By modeling the evolution of time series patterns, the method avoids unnecessary proliferation of clusters in a principled manner. We perform inference by formulating a variational lower bound for off-line and on-line scenarios, enabling efficient learning through optimization. We illustrate the versatility and effectiveness of the approach through several case studies of electrocardiogram analysis using publicly available databases.

Asymmetric Bidirectional Predictive Dynamics in Google Search and COVID-19 Time Series: A Multi-Horizon Forecast-Error Analysis

Abstract
Online search activity is widely used as a digital trace of information seeking during epidemics, but its predictive value is often reduced to correlation, a selected lag, or a single forecast-error score. We examine eight pre-specified Google Trends-COVID-19 pairs in both directions using matched LightGBM forecasts over rolling 400-day windows and horizons of 1-14 days. Log-RMSE gain is combined with forecast-error distribution entropy (FEDE), which captures horizon-dependent changes in the signed error distribution. The resulting directional predictive signature comprises response class, RMSE effect, FEDE endpoint and early-slope responses, persistence across rolling regimes, and horizon profile. For screening candidate directions, we also introduce a relative persistence-weighted directional predictive response strength (DPRS) on a within-study 0-100 scale; 100 denotes the strongest observed response in the comparison set, not an absolute coupling maximum. The pairs show concordant, RMSE-only, FEDE-only, and mixed responses. "loss of smell" -> reported cases is accuracy-dominant, with mean log-RMSE gain 0.104 (about 9.9% equivalent RMSE reduction) and DPRS 100, whereas the reverse direction is FEDE-dominant (D_FEDE=0.048; DPRS=42). "covid test" -> positive rate is FEDE-dominant (D_FEDE=0.039; positive in 72% of windows; DPRS=98) despite negative RMSE gain, while "lockdown" -> stringency is the clearest concordant case (DPRS=78). World-level symptom/case pairs are less persistent. Thus, forecast accuracy and error-distribution dynamics are complementary, predictive effects can be direction- and metric-dependent, and forecast horizon is part of the relationship. Aggregated regime-level patterns should not be extrapolated to individual time points or interpreted as evidence of causality.

Model- and Layer-Level Closed-Loop Forecasting with SSM-Based Spiking Neural Networks
Authors: Erik Stolt, Sanja Karilanova, Ayca Ozcelikkale
First: 2026-10-20T16:39:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T16:39:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Deep State-Space Models (Deep SSMs) have shown promising performance and computational benefits in processing of time-series data with complex temporal dependencies. Spiking neural networks (SNNs) constitute a biologically inspired class of deep SSMs, where computing blocks communicate through spikes, discrete single-bit events rather than continuous activation functions. A particularly important task in time-series processing is forecasting, i.e. predicting future values of a sequence from past observations alone. However, how to best perform forecasting with SNNs is an open question, where the standard autoregressive nature of the data processing with SNNs requires values that are not available during the forecasting phase. In this article, we address this challenge. We explore two closed-loop forecasting strategies, in which the network's own generated values are fed back into the network as if they were observed past values: (i) model-level and (ii) layer-level closed-loop forecasting. We evaluate these approaches on the ETT-small datasets, a standard benchmark for time-series forecasting, under both spike-based and continuous activation functions. We compare the proposed closed-loop approaches against state-of-the-art methods such as SpaceTime, as well as the zero-masking baseline in S4. Both of the proposed approaches perform comparably to or better than zero-masking with S4, and in most cases match SpaceTime. These findings highlight the trade-off between continuous and low-bit quantized activations, and demonstrate that the proposed Deep SSM architectures provide a competitive framework for forecasting.

Abstract
While data-driven techniques are powerful tools for reduced-order modeling of systems with chaotic dynamics, great potential remains for leveraging known physics (i.e. a full-order model (FOM)) to improve predictive capability. We develop a hybrid reduced order model (ROM), informed by both data and FOM, for evolving spatiotemporal chaotic dynamics on an invariant manifold whose coordinates are found using an autoencoder. This approach projects the vector field of the FOM onto the invariant manifold; then, this physics-derived vector field is either corrected using dynamic data, or used as a Bayesian prior that is updated with data. In both cases, the neural ordinary differential equation approach is used. We consider simulated data from the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and complex Ginzburg-Landau equations. Relative to the data-only approach, for scenarios of abundant data, scarce data, and even an incorrect FOM (i.e. erroneous parameter values), the hybrid approach yields substantially improved time-series predictions.

LightSplit: Practical Privacy-Preserving Split Learning via Orthogonal Projections (Full Version)
Authors: Mert Cihangiroglu, Alessandro Pegoraro, Phillip Rieger, Antonino Nocera, Ahmad-Reza Sadeghi
First: 2026-05-13T09:45:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T14:53:34+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the Network and Distributed System Security (NDSS) Symposium 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Split learning (SL) enables collaborative training by partitioning a neural network across clients and a central server. However, the cut-layer interface introduces a key challenge: high-dimensional activations incur substantial communication overhead while exposing representations vulnerable to reconstruction attacks. Existing approaches typically address efficiency or privacy in isolation. We propose LightSplit, which limits information exposure and reduces communication overhead via a lightweight fixed orthogonal random projection at the cut layer. Based on Shannon's information theory, this projection acts as an information bottleneck that restricts instance-specific information and suppresses exploitable per-sample signals. With low-dimensional projections sent instead of raw activations, the server operates on lifted representations without requiring changes to existing SL architectures. Adding no trainable client components, LightSplit remains lightweight and suitable for edge devices while preserving end-to-end differentiability via exact gradient propagation. As the projection is non-invertible, part of the original representation is irreversibly discarded at the client, so LightSplit reduces the information available for reconstruction. We evaluate LightSplit on seven benchmarks spanning image classification, tabular data, and language-model fine-tuning, under IID and non-IID client data. At up to 32x compression it retains at least 94% of baseline accuracy on CIFAR-10, FashionMNIST, GTSRB, and UCI HAR, 86% on CIFAR-100, and 91% on average under non-IID client data; at 8x it retains 92% of ImageNet-1k top-1 and raises GPT-2 perplexity by at most 1.15x. Against state-of-the-art reconstruction attacks LightSplit resists inversion; the strongest, co-trained attack loses up to 87.3% of its reconstruction SSIM, and subject re-identification on UCI HAR drops by more than half.

Split learning (SL) enables collaborative training by partitioning a neural network across clients and a central server.

Abstract
Accurate electrical load forecasting supports power-system operation across countries, regions, cities, and buildings, but repeated development of target-specific models is costly. Time series foundation models (TSFMs) provide reusable pretrained backbones, although multi-domain pretraining may not fully capture load-specific characteristics. This paper compares pretrained inference, full fine-tuning, and low-rank adaptation (LoRA) in terms of forecasting accuracy and adaptation efficiency across four electrical load aggregation levels. MOIRAI 1.0 Large serves as the primary backbone, while Chronos-2 assesses whether the findings transfer across model families. The evaluation covers both in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) settings and examines whether compact adaptation provides consistent gains over strong pretrained checkpoints. Pretrained foundation models outperform the implemented seasonal naive and XGBoost baselines across the evaluated aggregation levels. On MOIRAI Large, rank-16 LoRA reduces normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) by 7.5% on average while updating 3.146 million parameters, about 1% of the model, and retains most of the improvement from full fine-tuning. Chronos-2 achieves lower pretrained NMAE than MOIRAI but benefits less from adaptation. OOD results show that adaptation benefits vary by target, with the clearest gains for the local-area series and several buildings. Overall, LoRA provides a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off when adaptation yields a meaningful gain over a strong pretrained checkpoint.

Authors: Yibo Yang, Zerui Huang, Haomiao Yang, Zixia Wang
First: 2026-10-17T09:37:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T13:29:03+00:00

Abstract
Delirium affects a large fraction of intensive care unit (ICU) patients and foreshadows longer stays, lasting cognitive decline, and higher mortality. Flagging the patients who will become delirious within the first day of admission would give clinicians a window to intervene. Yet state-of-the-art predictors rely only on the structured electronic health record (EHR) of vital signs, laboratory values, and medications, while overlooking the free-text clinical notes written in the same window, which carry information the numbers cannot encode. To this end, we present DeliMF, a multimodal framework that fuses the structured time series with clinical text. It encodes the structured signals with a temporal Transformer and the time-stamped radiology reports with a clinical text encoder, and fuses the two through a gated cross-attention module. The gate learns a per-patient weight on the text; when a patient has no note the gate closes and DeliMF reduces to its structured-only form, giving robustness to missing text by construction. On an incident-only MIMIC-IV cohort, DeliMF improves significantly over the baselines, particularly on patients who carry clinical text, while losing nothing on those without. Ablation studies confirm the contribution of multimodal fusion and gated cross-attention, and a case study further shows that fusing clinical text with the structured stream recovers delirium cases that the structured data alone misses. We hope DeliMF encourages early ICU prediction to make fuller use of the overlooked clinical text and contributes to the early identification and timely intervention of delirium.

The surprising simplicity of forecasting quantum chaotic many-body dynamics
Authors: Francesco Perciavalle, Agostino Gallo, Francesco Plastina, Gianluigi Greco, Nicola Lo Gullo, Carlo Adornetto
First: 2026-10-07T18:00:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T08:43:35+00:00
Comments: Francesco Perciavalle and Agostino Gallo contributed equally to this work. 11 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Accurately simulating chaotic quantum many-body dynamics remains a major computational challenge for classical methods, due to the rapid buildup and spatial spreading of entanglement during the evolution. This raises the question of whether machine learning can provide an effective alternative for predicting quantum dynamics. We address this question by formulating quantum dynamics as a time-series forecasting problem, using a few-qubit PXP chain, realizable with Rydberg-atom arrays, as a benchmark. By varying the initial state, the system spans dynamical regimes ranging from ergodic behavior to quantum many-body scarring, providing a controlled setting for testing forecasting models across qualitatively different dynamics. We compare two contrasting architectures: an expressive nonlinear Transformer and DLinear, a simple linear forecasting model. The Transformer accurately predicts dynamics in the more ergodic regime, but its performance progressively deteriorates as the initial state approaches the scarred limit. In contrast, DLinear remains accurate across the entire family of initial states, with its main deviations consisting of small high-frequency oscillations that have little effect on the overall prediction error. Remarkably, these results show that observables generated by complex quantum many-body dynamics can be forecast with high accuracy through a simple linear mapping from past to future observations. This reveals that the complexity of the underlying quantum evolution need not translate into an equally complex forecasting problem.

Abstract
The rapid growth of distributed photovoltaic (PV) generation is increasing the demand for accurate forecasting across large numbers of geographically dispersed stations. Existing approaches are commonly developed for individual sites or specific forecasting horizons, requiring repeated training and maintenance as new stations are integrated and limiting direct reuse at previously unseen sites. To address this scalability gap, this paper presents a domain-specific foundation-style pretraining framework that learns a shared forecasting model from heterogeneous multi-site and multi-horizon PV data. NWP-assisted forecasting is formulated as a sequence-completion problem that unifies historical PV power, meteorological observations, future numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts, and time encodings. Three pretraining objectives are designed and systematically evaluated, including forecast-aligned reconstruction, generic reconstruction, and mixed reconstruction. The mixed objective combines the operational structure of PV forecasting with random sequence reconstruction and provides the most balanced performance across forecasting accuracy, degraded NWP conditions, and downstream adaptation. Experiments on 479 distributed PV stations use 340 stations for model development and 139 spatially held-out stations for zero-shot evaluation. Across horizons from 6 hours to 7 days, the multi-site pretrained MR model outperforms representative station-specific and shared baselines, as well as generalist time-series models, including a recent large-scale pretrained foundation model. The pretrained model can also be directly deployed at new PV stations and subsequently adapted to region-specific operational objectives when local data become available, providing a scalable forecasting paradigm for large distributed PV fleets.

Venue: IEEE Transactions on Image Processing, vol. 35, pp. 10972-10986, 2026

Abstract
In recent years, multi-view multi-label learning (MVML) has attracted extensive attention due to its close alignment to real-world scenarios. Information-theoretic methods have gained prominence for learning nonlinear correlations. However, two key challenges persist: first, features in real-world data commonly exhibit high-order structural correlations, but existing information-theoretic methods struggle to learn such correlations; second, commonly relying on heuristic optimization, information-theoretic methods are prone to converging to local optima. To address these two challenges, we propose a novel method called Structural Entropy Guided High-Order Correlation Learning for Multi-View Multi-Label Feature Selection (SEHFS). The core idea of SEHFS is to convert the feature graph into a structural-entropy-minimizing encoding tree, quantifying the information cost of high-order dependencies and thus learning high-order feature correlations beyond pairwise correlations. Specifically, features exhibiting strong high-order redundancy are grouped into a single cluster within the encoding tree, while inter-cluster feaeture correlations are minimized, thereby eliminating redundancy both within and across clusters. Furthermore, a new framework based on the fusion of information theory and matrix methods is adopted, which learns a shared semantic matrix and view-specific contribution matrices to reconstruct a global view matrix, thereby enhancing the information-theoretic method and balancing the global and local optimization. The ability of structural entropy to learn high-order correlations is theoretically established, and and both experiments on eight datasets from various domains and ablation studies demonstrate that SEHFS achieves superior performance in feature selection.

Abstract
Post-hoc residual correction can reduce forecasting error, but always-on updates often fail to generalize: when residual signals are weak or noisy, expressive correctors can overfit and even increase the overall forecasting errors. We introduce CRC, a robust residual correction framework that couples a hybrid corrector with a validation-calibrated selective deployment rule. CRC proposes corrections via a conservative ridge "floor" plus conditional nonlinear refinement, but deploys them only when held-out evidence indicates net error reduction over a frozen baseline forecaster. CRC treats when to revise a forecast as a validation-calibrated deployment decision, not only how to fit residuals. Across standard long-sequence benchmarks and multiple backbones, CRC improves horizon-averaged MSE and MAE in most dataset-backbone pairs, with particularly large gains on complex datasets (e.g., over 20% relative MSE reduction on Traffic). We additionally report the non-degradation rate (NDR) as a stability diagnostic of harmful updates and ablate the validation-calibrated "firewall" that stabilizes MSE gains.

Abstract
As cognitive radars evolve towards the end-to-end processing of one-dimensional complex baseband sequences, deep networks face severe adversarial threats. However, existing attacks encounter dual limitations in practical electronic warfare: low cross-architecture transferability and severe vulnerability to physical channel distortions, which degrades their tactical reliability. Abstract
Adapting a forecasting system to a changing world classically means retraining. We ask whether the task of continually learning regimes for time series forecasting can be represented by a piece of text: a guidance block, evolved by outcome-driven reflection and used in a frozen LLM forecaster's prompt. On a refreshed multimodal benchmark (weekly search indices, daily currencies, daily commodities, each window paired with web-retrieved news), we run GEPA-style prompt evolution under strict contamination control to prevent the data leakage common in timeseries benchmarks: the forecaster's knowledge cutoff predates every evaluated window. A 900-word evolved guidance carried by Llama-3.3-70B beats TimeSeries Foundation Models (TSFMs) on held-out data (GM MASE 0.776 vs. 0.898 for TabPFN-TS-3), transfers to more recent models without measurable loss, and costs dollars rather than GPU-days to update. We characterize the loop: out-of-loop gains concentrate in the first reflection, in-loop improvements overstate out-of-loop gains 2-3x unless an untouched monitor arbitrates, and error-only objectives silently suppress the text modality. We provide two extensions. First, compiling the guidance into executable code with a TSFM as a callable tool recovers most of the gain deterministically. Second, ablations show the advantage widens at short horizons and that removing the news text does not hurt the guided forecaster, consistent with the text's value being distilled into policy during learning. We argue that continual prompt evolution is a practical, training-free alternative to retraining for multimodal forecasting.

Abstract

Abstract
Analyzing merchant transaction histories requires dynamic temporal filtering, entity resolution, and aggregation over irregular event streams--operations poorly captured by static tabular or time-series forecasting benchmarks. We introduce TxnQA, an analytical QA benchmark comprising 27 task families (81 templates) alongside a 5-scenario fraud-risk reasoning suite that evaluates both verdicts and supporting evidence over synthetic, privacy-mitigated transaction data. Abstract
Generative models for multivariate time series are routinely evaluated with pooled distributional metrics computed after marginal calibration. We show that, under a held-out protocol, the calibrated Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) distance differs from a real-versus-real floor by at most the distance of the calibrated sample to the training block, so that it carries little information about the generator. We audit a three-discriminator GAN with evolutionary weight adaptation (StatD2GAN) against re-implemented RCGAN and TimeGAN baselines on ERA5 weather sequences under a pre-specified protocol: the final two calendar years are held out behind a 168 hour embargo, calibration is fitted on the training block only, and the decision rules were frozen in advance. Evidence comes from 25 matched (location, seed) pairs across five locations, tested with Holm-corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. First, isotonic calibration drives KS to a median of 1.019 times a per-location noise-and-shift floor, and neither baseline is distinguishable from StatD2GAN on it. Second, the sorted-representation discriminator is the only component whose removal significantly degrades cross-variable dependence (Kendall tau MAE +0.116, Holm p = 0.002); a rank-transformed variant also degrades it, consistent with quantile supervision rather than copula matching. Third, calibration raises physical constraint violations; projection removes them, but the largest marginal change (0.0110, Lhasa) exceeds the pre-specified tolerance in three locations. Fourth, the re-implemented TimeGAN is significantly better than StatD2GAN on Kendall tau and ACF MAE, RCGAN is worse on Kendall tau MAE and constraint violations, and the pre-specified collapse criterion was not met. We recommend floor-referenced marginal evaluation, matched-pair testing, and dependence and sequence-level metrics.

Abstract
Learning latent dynamics is central to assessing the current states and forecasting the future trajectories of high-dimensional time series. For locally-stationary latent dynamics $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{z}_{<t}; \mathbf{c})$ with latent dynamics state $\mathbf{z}_t$ and environment variable $\mathbf{c}$, prior identifiability results have largely focused on $\mathbf{z}_t$ when conditioned on predefined label $u$ of dynamics environments. This leaves two limitations: reliance on predefined labels that hinder generalization to unseen environments, and a limited understanding of the identifiability of $\mathcal{F}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ which -- although offering important structural properties for the identifiability of $\mathbf{z}_t$ -- are learned jointly with $\mathbf{z}_t$. We address these limitations with Meta-iLaD, a novel identifiable latent dynamics framework attained by meta-learning across dynamics environments. Meta-iLaD introduces a novel conditional prior of $\mathbf{c}$, modeled as a feedforward meta-learner to rapidly extract $\mathbf{c}$ from few-shot examples. Meta-iLaD further establishes identifiability for $\mathbf{z}_t$, $\mathbf{c}$, and $\mathcal{F}$ simultaneously, for a general formulation of $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{z}_{< t};\mathbf{c})$ without restricting the dimension of $\mathbf{c}$ or how it modulates $\mathcal{F}$. On synthetic and real data, we provide strong empirical evidence that 1) conditioning on few-shot examples enables generalization to out-of-distribution environments, and 2) identifiability for $\mathbf{c}$ and $\mathcal{F}$ is critical for accurate forecasting beyond reconstructing observed trajectories. Abstract
Multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF) is critical across many real-world domains. Existing deep learning approaches fall into two paradigms with distinct limitations: channel-independent (CI) methods unconditionally ignore cross-variable dependencies and model only temporal dynamics, while channel-dependent (CD) methods consider both but typically rely on architectural compromises to mitigate overfitting and computational overhead. We therefore propose Chameleon, a specialized CD state space model (SSM) that enables data-dependent, fine-grained interactions across variables while scaling linearly with their number. On strongly dependent ODE and PEMS datasets, Chameleon achieves the best MSE and MAE across all settings, while its CI ablation and prior CD methods incur 61-178% higher MSE on average. Across 28 standard benchmark settings, Chameleon also achieves better MSE and MAE than each baseline in at least 27 and 22 cases, respectively. Training-time and peak-memory analyses on Traffic and ETT further demonstrate competitive efficiency and favorable memory scalability across different variable counts.

Abstract
Recent time series foundation models (TSFMs) can consume covariates in-context, extending zero-shot forecasting to problems driven by external factors. This enables scenario analysis: forecasting how a target responds to events that set covariates to chosen future values. Whether they can do this reliably is an open question. We introduce WhatIf-Bench, a benchmark measuring how well forecasters use covariates to forecast a target's response to future events. WhatIf-Bench is semi-synthetic: time series come from mechanistic simulators of data centers, power grids, road traffic, and water networks, with controlled covariate-target relationships grounded in each system. Each task presents a history of events intervening on covariates, then asks to forecast a future event that either replays seen values (Replay) or sets them to unseen ones (Novel). Varying the number of covariates, historical events, and event length yields 37,665 tasks. Evaluating TSFMs, frontier LLMs, and simple regression baselines, we find the LLMs reach the highest skill (improvement over a naive baseline), ahead of the best TSFMs. Every model forecasts well on Replay futures (48 to 85% skill) but drops to -7 to 50% on Novel futures, with the regression baselines falling the most. Skill on Novel futures also falls as events set more covariates jointly. More historical events raise skill on Novel futures across the board, but large differences remain. WhatIf-Bench reveals generalization to Novel futures as the core challenge, and offers a testbed toward arbitrary scenario analysis.

Abstract
Due to the diversity of real-world time series, no single forecasting model consistently dominates across all samples. Ensemble learning addresses this by combining complementary model strengths, yet existing methods rely on fixed rules or black-box models based solely on numerical inputs, failing to leverage LLM reasoning for interpretable weighting decisions. We propose REATS, which leverages LLM reasoning capabilities as an intelligent ensemble router that jointly processes textual temporal pattern descriptions and numerical features to produce interpretable, sample-adaptive ensemble weights through chain-of-thought reasoning. To enable effective LLM-based ensembling, we study its key design choices and propose: (i) a structured input pipeline that transforms raw time series into hybrid textual--numerical representations with fixed token cost, enabling rule-based chain-of-thought construction without API dependency, augmented with retrieved similar-sample priors; (ii) a diverse multi-row weight supervision scheme coupled with a token-efficient percentage-table format that reduces numerical complexity and mitigates LLM hallucinations; and (iii) a two-stage fine-tuning framework combining SFT with GRPO, where a reciprocal reward mapping transforms the continuous unbounded MSE gap into bounded signals with amplified near-oracle sensitivity, addressing the uniform sensitivity and outlier-dominated advantage compression inherent in naive reward designs for regression-based GRPO. Experiments on eight benchmarks demonstrate that REATS outperforms competitive ensemble baselines while providing natural language explanations and demonstrating strong transfer learning and out-of-domain generalization to unseen candidate models.

Abstract
Irregular multivariate time series forecasting (IMTSF) requires capturing temporal dynamics and cross-variable dependencies from sparse, asynchronous observations. Existing methods often rely on learned temporal encoders, coupling historical representation construction with model optimisation and repeatedly processing the same observations. We propose CTLite, a lightweight forecasting framework that decouples historical summarisation from learned prediction. Specifically, a parameter-free continuous-time moment encoder summarises local levels and trends at multiple temporal scales while suppressing poorly supported slope estimates. A compact readout combines damped local extrapolation with a low-rank cross-variable correction that accounts for observation freshness, target availability, and forecast horizon. Since the historical summaries are independent of trainable parameters and forecast queries, they can be reused across optimisation steps and repeated predictions for an unchanged history without altering predictions or parameter gradients. Experiments on four real-world benchmarks show that CTLite achieves the best or second-best mean MSE and MAE among the evaluated methods with only 88-2,537 trainable parameters. Under matched GPU workloads at batch size 32, CTLite reduces inference latency by 18.6-87.3% compared with the latest models, including the cost of historical encoding. Reusing summaries further reduces total runtime by approximately 52% over 16 forecast calls on the same history compared with recomputing the encoder for every call, including the initial encoding cost. Abstract
Static public benchmarks for time series foundation models (TSFMs) typically have fixed and narrow test distributions. Their repeated reuse encourages benchmark-specific tuning, making reported performance reflect adaptation to the fixed test set rather than genuine forecasting generalization. In addition, a single aggregate score cannot fully characterize model capabilities across distinct temporal structures and input relationships. We propose CaFE (Capability-Focused Evaluation), a refreshable evaluation extension grounded in original benchmark data that enriches static benchmarks with structural diversity and enriches single-score black-box evaluation with multi-dimensional, capability-oriented assessment. CaFE first profiles the original benchmark and defines eight Structural Features (SFs) that characterize its temporal structure. For each SF, it resamples the original data according to its structural support, removes edge-of-distribution samples, and augments the retained samples by varying the SF's salience, producing samples with different structural profiles. After filtering out out-of-distribution samples while preserving diversity, CaFE yields a refreshed evaluation set. TSFMs are then evaluated on this set using both conventional forecast-error metrics and intervention-response metrics, giving each model a per-feature capability profile alongside endpoint accuracy. Experiments on GIFT-Eval and FEV-Mini20 show that CaFE yields stable evaluation results and reveals per-feature strengths and weaknesses that a single aggregate score hides. Moreover, on genuine data filtered by structural condition, models selected by CaFE outperform those chosen by the aggregate leaderboard. CaFE therefore makes model selection conditional on the structural response of interest.

Abstract
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) have achieved notable progress in classification tasks through large-scale pretraining. However, their post-training practice mainly focuses on implicit parameter fine-tuning, where task-relevant information is entangled with abundant irrelevant knowledge in vast parameter spaces. This may hinder TSFMs from precisely activating useful knowledge for downstream classification. To this end, we propose Retrieval-to-Recognition (R2R), a plug-and-play post-training paradigm that adapts pretrained TSFMs to time series classification. Rather than pretraining a new foundation model, R2R improves how existing TSFMs are post-trained: it introduces a decoupled query encoder to extract discriminative patterns and retrieve instance-level numerical knowledge from a constructed knowledge base. Abstract
Deep generative models provide an effective modern solution to alleviating the problem of data scarcity by synthesizing realistic time series. However, despite the considerable progress achieved by existing deep time series generators, two key challenges remain: (1) learning hierarchical representations for heterogeneous temporal patterns; and (2) coordinating the generation process of heterogeneous temporal dynamics. To address these key bottlenecks, in this work, we propose ORBIT, a unified deep generative framework for time series. Abstract
Echo State Networks (ESNs), as lightweight implementations of reservoir computing, provide efficient solutions for temporal data processing in resource-constrained environments. While standard ESNs offer computational efficiency, their dense readout layers inflate the stored model and the per-sample inference cost. This paper introduces a Smooth-L1 regularized ESN (SL1ESN) that promotes sparsity in the readout layer while preserving differentiability for gradient-based optimization. The method employs a twice-differentiable Smooth-L1 approximation compatible with Newton's method, which we exploit both for centralized training and for a second-order (Newton-step) federated aggregation scheme. Under a single fixed shrinkage threshold shared by every dataset and both settings, experiments on seven time-series classification benchmarks show that SL1ESN learns readouts that are $77$--$95\%$ sparse in the centralized setting and $75$--$95\%$ sparse in the federated one, while staying within $0.7$ accuracy points of the strongest dense baseline on five of the seven benchmarks in the centralized setting and within $1.6$ points on the same five in the federated one, and the second-order federated update converges in only a few rounds. The resulting sparsity compresses the stored model, the per-sample inference cost, and the server-to-client broadcast by a comparable factor. These results demonstrate the practical value of sparse readouts for edge computing and IoT deployments.
