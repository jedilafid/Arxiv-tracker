Channel-Dependent State Space Model for Multivariate Time Series Forecasting
Authors: Yu-Cheng Wu, Fan-Keng Sun, Li-Chun Lu, Duane S. Boning
First: 2026-09-29T01:15:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:14:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multivariate time series forecasting (MTSF) is critical across many real-world domains. Existing deep learning approaches fall into two paradigms with distinct limitations: channel-independent (CI) methods unconditionally ignore cross-variable dependencies and model only temporal dynamics, while channel-dependent (CD) methods consider both but typically rely on architectural compromises to mitigate overfitting and computational overhead. We therefore propose Chameleon, a specialized CD state space model (SSM) that enables data-dependent, fine-grained interactions across variables while scaling linearly with their number. By connecting selective SSMs with the Kalman filter, we leverage the missing measurement update in the former for cross-variable modeling while preserving the SSM backbone for robust temporal modeling. We further identify favorable inductive biases of GatedDeltaNet for time series, adapt it as our backbone, and improve generalization through additional techniques, including a previously unexplored stochastic perturbation of reversible instance normalization. On strongly dependent ODE and PEMS datasets, Chameleon achieves the best MSE and MAE across all settings, while its CI ablation and prior CD methods incur 61-178% higher MSE on average. Across 28 standard benchmark settings, Chameleon also achieves better MSE and MAE than each baseline in at least 27 and 22 cases, respectively. Training-time and peak-memory analyses on Traffic and ETT further demonstrate competitive efficiency and favorable memory scalability across different variable counts.

Abstract
Recent time series foundation models (TSFMs) can consume covariates in-context, extending zero-shot forecasting to problems driven by external factors. This enables scenario analysis: forecasting how a target responds to events that set covariates to chosen future values. Whether they can do this reliably is an open question. We introduce WhatIf-Bench, a benchmark measuring how well forecasters use covariates to forecast a target's response to future events. WhatIf-Bench is semi-synthetic: time series come from mechanistic simulators of data centers, power grids, road traffic, and water networks, with controlled covariate-target relationships grounded in each system. Each task presents a history of events intervening on covariates, then asks to forecast a future event that either replays seen values (Replay) or sets them to unseen ones (Novel). Varying the number of covariates, historical events, and event length yields 37,665 tasks. Evaluating TSFMs, frontier LLMs, and simple regression baselines, we find the LLMs reach the highest skill (improvement over a naive baseline), ahead of the best TSFMs. Every model forecasts well on Replay futures (48 to 85% skill) but drops to -7 to 50% on Novel futures, with the regression baselines falling the most. Skill on Novel futures also falls as events set more covariates jointly. More historical events raise skill on Novel futures across the board, but large differences remain. WhatIf-Bench reveals generalization to Novel futures as the core challenge, and offers a testbed toward arbitrary scenario analysis.

REATS: LLM Reasoning-based Ensemble Learning for Adaptive Time Series Forecasting
Authors: Xu Zhang, Chang Xu, Hui Sun, Nan Ma, Zijian Zhang, Peng Wang, Wei Wang, Li Zhao
First: 2026-08-10T19:04:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T14:52:03+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Due to the diversity of real-world time series, no single forecasting model consistently dominates across all samples. Ensemble learning addresses this by combining complementary model strengths, yet existing methods rely on fixed rules or black-box models based solely on numerical inputs, failing to leverage LLM reasoning for interpretable weighting decisions. We propose REATS, which leverages LLM reasoning capabilities as an intelligent ensemble router that jointly processes textual temporal pattern descriptions and numerical features to produce interpretable, sample-adaptive ensemble weights through chain-of-thought reasoning. To enable effective LLM-based ensembling, we study its key design choices and propose: (i) a structured input pipeline that transforms raw time series into hybrid textual--numerical representations with fixed token cost, enabling rule-based chain-of-thought construction without API dependency, augmented with retrieved similar-sample priors; (ii) a diverse multi-row weight supervision scheme coupled with a token-efficient percentage-table format that reduces numerical complexity and mitigates LLM hallucinations; and (iii) a two-stage fine-tuning framework combining SFT with GRPO, where a reciprocal reward mapping transforms the continuous unbounded MSE gap into bounded signals with amplified near-oracle sensitivity, addressing the uniform sensitivity and outlier-dominated advantage compression inherent in naive reward designs for regression-based GRPO. Experiments on eight benchmarks demonstrate that REATS outperforms competitive ensemble baselines while providing natural language explanations and demonstrating strong transfer learning and out-of-domain generalization to unseen candidate models.

CTLite: Lightweight Irregular Multivariate Time Series Forecasting with Reusable Continuous-Time Summaries
Authors: Shusen Ma, Xudong Zuo, Yangchun Xie, Yun-Bo Zhao
First: 2026-10-19T13:32:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T13:32:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Irregular multivariate time series forecasting (IMTSF) requires capturing temporal dynamics and cross-variable dependencies from sparse, asynchronous observations. Existing methods often rely on learned temporal encoders, coupling historical representation construction with model optimisation and repeatedly processing the same observations. We propose CTLite, a lightweight forecasting framework that decouples historical summarisation from learned prediction. Specifically, a parameter-free continuous-time moment encoder summarises local levels and trends at multiple temporal scales while suppressing poorly supported slope estimates. A compact readout combines damped local extrapolation with a low-rank cross-variable correction that accounts for observation freshness, target availability, and forecast horizon. Since the historical summaries are independent of trainable parameters and forecast queries, they can be reused across optimisation steps and repeated predictions for an unchanged history without altering predictions or parameter gradients. Experiments on four real-world benchmarks show that CTLite achieves the best or second-best mean MSE and MAE among the evaluated methods with only 88-2,537 trainable parameters. Under matched GPU workloads at batch size 32, CTLite reduces inference latency by 18.6-87.3% compared with the latest models, including the cost of historical encoding. Reusing summaries further reduces total runtime by approximately 52% over 16 forecast calls on the same history compared with recomputing the encoder for every call, including the initial encoding cost. Beyond Static Leaderboards: Capability-Focused Evaluation with Structural Diversity for Time Series Foundation Models
Authors: Hongyin Zhang, Mingyi Xiang, Rongzhao Chen, Yankai Wang, Jialin Qiao, Xiangdong Huang, Xiaoou Ding, Chen Wang, Zhongyi Pei, Jianmin Wang
First: 2026-10-19T12:10:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T12:10:42+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Static public benchmarks for time series foundation models (TSFMs) typically have fixed and narrow test distributions. Their repeated reuse encourages benchmark-specific tuning, making reported performance reflect adaptation to the fixed test set rather than genuine forecasting generalization. In addition, a single aggregate score cannot fully characterize model capabilities across distinct temporal structures and input relationships. We propose CaFE (Capability-Focused Evaluation), a refreshable evaluation extension grounded in original benchmark data that enriches static benchmarks with structural diversity and enriches single-score black-box evaluation with multi-dimensional, capability-oriented assessment. CaFE first profiles the original benchmark and defines eight Structural Features (SFs) that characterize its temporal structure. For each SF, it resamples the original data according to its structural support, removes edge-of-distribution samples, and augments the retained samples by varying the SF's salience, producing samples with different structural profiles. After filtering out out-of-distribution samples while preserving diversity, CaFE yields a refreshed evaluation set. TSFMs are then evaluated on this set using both conventional forecast-error metrics and intervention-response metrics, giving each model a per-feature capability profile alongside endpoint accuracy. Experiments on GIFT-Eval and FEV-Mini20 show that CaFE yields stable evaluation results and reveals per-feature strengths and weaknesses that a single aggregate score hides. Moreover, on genuine data filtered by structural condition, models selected by CaFE outperform those chosen by the aggregate leaderboard. CaFE therefore makes model selection conditional on the structural response of interest.

Forecasting Voltage Time Series under Anomalies Using a WGAN Model With Lipschitz Control and Progressive Kernel Critic Strategy
Authors: Najmieh Sadat Safarabadi
First: 2026-10-16T12:52:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T11:30:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In time-series forecasting tasks, the use of recursive forecasting on high-dimensional data substantially increases the complexity of the prediction process. The main challenge is that long-term dynamics becomes harder to model, since the sequences show greater temporal variability. To counteract this limitation and improve the modeling of long-term dynamics, and more importantly, address high-dimensional recursive forecasting, this paper uses a Wasserstein GAN equipped with a progressive Lipschitz penalty and an adaptive kernel. This model is capable of taking a window of the data stream and forecasting the next values, subsequently reconstructing the time-series stream and dynamically adapting its kernel to each new window. The proposed model is a deep learning framework based on a Wasserstein GAN (WGAN-CLP) with an adaptive kernel to model alterations in streaming voltage time series. To establish the methodological foundation, we review the WGAN-LP formulation another variant model in this class and extend its framework through an adaptive kernel critic model. We then evaluate this extended model using power ratios, correlation coefficient (CC), and relative root mean squared error (RRMSE). The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms benchmark methods in both accuracy and robustness. The evaluation shows promising performance of the WGAN-CLP with an adaptive kernel framework in forecasting sequence alterations across the voltage and energy time-series datasets.

From Retrieval to Recognition:Knowledge-Enhanced Post-training of Time Series Foundation Models for Classification

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Time series foundation models (TSFMs) have achieved notable progress in classification tasks through large-scale pretraining. However, their post-training practice mainly focuses on implicit parameter fine-tuning, where task-relevant information is entangled with abundant irrelevant knowledge in vast parameter spaces. This may hinder TSFMs from precisely activating useful knowledge for downstream classification. To this end, we propose Retrieval-to-Recognition (R2R), a plug-and-play post-training paradigm that adapts pretrained TSFMs to time series classification. Rather than pretraining a new foundation model, R2R improves how existing TSFMs are post-trained: it introduces a decoupled query encoder to extract discriminative patterns and retrieve instance-level numerical knowledge from a constructed knowledge base. The retrieved explicit knowledge is then fused with the TSFM's implicit parametric embeddings, enabling joint knowledge transfer at both the parameter and feature levels. In this way, R2R allows TSFMs to recall task-relevant patterns learned during pretraining, improving the efficiency of knowledge utilization in downstream classification transfer. Experiments on 158 time series datasets show that R2R consistently improves the classification performance of state-of-the-art TSFMs. Ablation and knowledge-enhanced studies further validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework.

Abstract
Echo State Networks (ESNs), as lightweight implementations of reservoir computing, provide efficient solutions for temporal data processing in resource-constrained environments. While standard ESNs offer computational efficiency, their dense readout layers inflate the stored model and the per-sample inference cost. This paper introduces a Smooth-L1 regularized ESN (SL1ESN) that promotes sparsity in the readout layer while preserving differentiability for gradient-based optimization. The method employs a twice-differentiable Smooth-L1 approximation compatible with Newton's method, which we exploit both for centralized training and for a second-order (Newton-step) federated aggregation scheme. Under a single fixed shrinkage threshold shared by every dataset and both settings, experiments on seven time-series classification benchmarks show that SL1ESN learns readouts that are $77$--$95\%$ sparse in the centralized setting and $75$--$95\%$ sparse in the federated one, while staying within $0.7$ accuracy points of the strongest dense baseline on five of the seven benchmarks in the centralized setting and within $1.6$ points on the same five in the federated one, and the second-order federated update converges in only a few rounds. The resulting sparsity compresses the stored model, the per-sample inference cost, and the server-to-client broadcast by a comparable factor. These results demonstrate the practical value of sparse readouts for edge computing and IoT deployments.

Accelerated Drift of Scientific Prose in the LLM Era: Evidence from an Interrupted Time-Series Analysis
Authors: Hanna Hubarava, Benjamin V. Ineichen
Venue: EMNLP 2026
First: 2026-10-19T08:33:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T08:33:10+00:00
Comments: Accepted at EMNLP 2026. 22 pages, including references and appendices
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Every scientific text is embedded in the writing norms of its time: it reflects earlier publications while also shaping the texts that follow. The growing use of LLM-based writing tools introduces a new factor in this evolving system, but the linguistic changes coinciding with their emergence remain insufficiently understood. We investigate whether the trajectories of scientific-preprint writing changed in rate or direction after conversational LLMs became publicly accessible, using the release of ChatGPT in November 2022 as the observational breakpoint. We compare pre- and post-2022 trajectories across vocabulary, punctuation, readability, and syntax in abstracts from arXiv's artificial intelligence, statistics, and quantitative biology categories and from medRxiv. Using interrupted time-series regression, we find substantial post-2022 changes across multiple feature families and research domains, including increases in average word length, reduced formula-based readability, and declines in maximum dependency-tree depth and sentence-level depth variation. Individual features follow different dynamics: some lexical markers accelerate persistently, while others rise and then recede. Our findings show that isolated marker frequencies capture only part of the shift: changes in the rate, direction, and structure of linguistic trajectories provide complementary evidence of broader post-2022 change in scientific-preprint writing. More broadly, these findings motivate monitoring scientific writing as a multidimensional, evolving system rather than through a fixed set of LLM-associated markers.

Abstract
Time-series analysis is increasingly studied in multimodal settings, yet most benchmarks pair temporal signals with text or with visualizations derived from the same signal. This leaves open whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can integrate time series with real-world visual evidence. We introduce MMTA (multimodal temporal analysis), a benchmark of tri-modal tasks with diverse cross-modal complementarity patterns among time series, text, and vision. MMTA spans nine domains -- robotics, autonomous driving, human action, manufacturing, AR assistance, healthcare, recommendation, finance, and climate -- under video+TS and image+TS layouts, with a 3,524-sample test split covering classification and forecasting. The benchmark standardizes heterogeneous real-world sources into a shared schema with domain-specific prompts, provenance documentation, and success-weighted metrics that penalize both wrong answers and failed generations. Evaluating eight open-source and proprietary MLLMs reveals a shared bottleneck: representing time series as text creates very long token sequences and weakens fine-grained alignment with visual evidence, especially in long-video settings. We then introduce TimeOmni-v, a multimodal LLM extension with a dedicated time-series encoder, temporal positional alignment, modality-aware token layout, and a forecasting head. TimeOmni-v improves long-video classification and time-series inference efficiency, showing that MMTA not only exposes representation and grounding failures but also motivates targeted model design.

TimesJev: Thinking Fast on Time Series with System 1 Sufficiency
Authors: Chenghao Liu, Wei Fan, Jindong Han, Dongjie Wang, Aniket Mahanti, Pengyang Wang, Hao Liu
First: 2026-10-19T08:26:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T08:26:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) have recently shown strong potential for time series reasoning, while models that reason over time series are usually with long chains of thought. However, this process often consumes thousands of tokens, directly leading to substantial latency during inference. In contrast, System 1 models such as Jev make decisions instantly and can naturally be an alternative, but raw time series are not compatible with the embedding space of these models, thus hindering the reasoning performance, especially when input series contain thousands of numeric points. In this paper, we make a very early attempt to propose a time series System 1 model, namely TimesJev. Specifically, we first formalize System 1 sufficiency in time series reasoning, which motivates a compressed representation for time series with key frequency patterns preserved. To construct this representation, we derive a dimension reduction objective that combines (i) a sufficiency divergence that measures the core information lost during compression, and (ii) a realizability divergence that quantifies the incompatibility of the System 1 model to the optimal prediction given this representation. We optimize this objective by learning time series compression with a gating mechanism for sufficiency, and then aligning the selected representation with the embedding space for realizability. Finally, experiments on 13 datasets demonstrate that TimesJev outperforms reasoning models by over 62% with 62.9% less inference time than their fine-tuned versions on average.

Abstract
Automated incident management in large-scale microservice systems relies on learning robust representations from multimodal observability data, including metrics, logs, and traces. Although recent self-supervised frameworks enable unified modeling for anomaly detection (AD), failure triage (FT), and root cause localization (RCL), they often struggle with non-stationary temporal dynamics and heterogeneous service dependency structures. In this paper, we propose STAR, a Spatial-Temporal Adaptive Representation learning framework that explicitly addresses these challenges through adaptive normalizations. STAR introduces two tightly coupled mechanisms: Temporal Adaptive Normalization (TAN), which dynamically normalizes multivariate time series using multi-scale temporal context, and Spatial Adaptive Normalization (SAN), which performs structure-aware normalization over service dependency graphs. Unlike prior methods that treat normalization as static or task-agnostic, STAR formulates it as a learnable, context-conditioned transformation aligned with the intrinsic properties of microservice systems. The resulting adaptive representations are integrated into a unified self-supervised framework, enabling end-to-end unsupervised support for AD, FT, and RCL tasks. Extensive experiments on two real-world microservice benchmarks demonstrate that STAR consistently outperforms all state-of-the-art baselines, yielding significant and stable improvements across all three tasks. Our results highlight adaptive normalization as a principled and effective mechanism for robust multimodal representation learning in complex software systems.

Abstract
Dynamic edge classification on text-attributed Web graphs, such as review networks, community forums, and news-derived knowledge graphs, requires high quality, adaptive semantic representations in the context of network evolution. Existing dynamic graph architectures typically initialize text feature vectors with pretrained language models and hold them static during training to preserve their semantic structure, forgoing the potential benefits of allowing them to co-evolve with network topology. In this work, we investigate the effects of enabling such co-evolution with a unified text-graph alignment framework that integrates a lightweight Transformer adapter to update token-level text representations via spatio-temporal gradients from a contrastive pretraining objective. To prevent detrimental semantic distortion, a language model maintenance objective anchors the evolving text embeddings to their original semantic structure. Representational analyses reveal that the framework reduces anisotropy, improving the linear separability of text embeddings, and effectively encodes structural and temporal information into the semantic space. A computational efficiency analysis confirms that this beneficial semantic and spatio-temporal co-evolution is achieved without incurring the prohibitive computational overhead typical of end-to-end joint training.

ActTime: Action-Conditioned World Modeling for Time Series
Authors: Xinxing Zhou, Xiaojie Yuan, Yanlong Wen, Ming Jin
First: 2026-10-19T04:24:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T04:24:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Time-series models increasingly condition on known future inputs. In controlled systems, some of these inputs are actions that can be changed, compared, and optimized before execution. A model must therefore do more than predict the realized future: it must predict how outcomes change under different actions from the same observed history and support reliable comparison of candidate plans. We introduce ActTime, an action-conditioned world model for time series. Over the observed history, ActTime updates variable-specific latent states through an action-conditioned transition and corrects them using observations. It then rolls the same transition forward under candidate actions to generate future trajectories. Training uses only factual trajectories, without paired alternative-action outcomes or planning supervision. We construct a simulator-grounded evaluation suite across building energy, traffic, greenhouse climate, and inventory management, covering factual prediction, intervention-effect prediction, offline decision making, and closed-loop planning. ActTime ranks first in 12 of 16 domain-task settings. Across eight methods, the mean pairwise model-ranking reversal rate is 17.8% between factual prediction and intervention fidelity, and rises to 28.4% between intervention fidelity and decision ranking. These results show that factual accuracy, intervention fidelity, and decision quality are related but not interchangeable.

Time-series models increasingly condition on known future inputs.
Interpretable time series analysis with Gumbel dynamics
Authors: Yiliu Wang, Timothy Doyeon Kim, Eric Shea-Brown, Uygar Sümbül
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2025-09-25T20:56:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T03:10:52+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Switching dynamical systems can model complicated time series data while maintaining interpretability by inferring a finite set of dynamics primitives and explaining different portions of the observed time series with one of these primitives. However, due to the discrete nature of this set, such models struggle to capture smooth, variable-speed transitions, as well as stochastic mixtures of overlapping states (e.g., non-instantaneous state transitions), and the inferred dynamics often display spurious rapid switching on real-world datasets. Here, we propose the Gumbel Dynamical Model (GDM). First, by introducing a continuous relaxation of discrete states and a different noise model defined on the relaxed-discrete state space via the Gumbel distribution, GDM expands the set of available state dynamics, allowing the model to approximate smoother and non-stationary ground-truth dynamics more faithfully. Breaking from established literature, this new class uses a stochastic relaxed state to directly combine reusable dynamical primitives and govern observations, without a separate stochastic continuous trajectory. Second, the relaxation makes the model fully differentiable, enabling fast and scalable training with standard gradient descent methods. We validate our approach on standard simulation datasets and highlight its ability to model soft, sticky states and transitions in a stochastic setting. Furthermore, we apply our model to two real-world datasets, demonstrating its ability to infer interpretable states in stochastic time series with multiple dynamics, a setting where traditional methods often fail.

Abstract
Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) are becoming a core representation-learning primitive and a building block for latent world models across vision, video, audio, brain dynamics, and time series. Despite (potential of) wide deployment, current JEPA training pipelines are inefficient: each input is executed through multiple mask-specific branches, with redundant target-side work, and memory-bound token routing. These results show that execution restructuring, rather than changes to the JEPA objective, is a key lever for efficient JEPA training.

Abstract
Time series are ubiquitous across different domains, and the growing use of synthetic data for privacy-preserving sharing, data augmentation, and stress testing makes time series generation increasingly important. Early generative approaches are predominantly autoregressive, which can suffer from low sample diversity, error accumulation, and difficulty modeling long-range dependencies.
